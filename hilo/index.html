    How to play

    Twelve names, eleven calls. You see one number. Is the next one higher or lower? On a board of years the faces read earlier or later; on a board of ages, younger or older. The question names who the call is about and what it is against, every time.

    A clock on every call. Each call is worth up to ten. The clock holds for two seconds so you can read it, then the points fall a point a second to nothing. A call that runs out is a wrong call, and waits for you to tap Next; an answered call moves straight on.

    Three substitutions. A wrong call spends one. Three wrong and it is still a win; four is a draw. Leave a board unfinished and it is a loss.

    114 is the ceiling. Eleven right at full value is 110, and every run of five right in a row is worth two more. Two runs of five, or a perfect eleven, reach 114.

    The source shows as the call settles. Every number on a board comes from an opened official record, and the line that proves it appears beside the answer once you have called it, never before.
